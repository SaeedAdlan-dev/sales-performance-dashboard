# 02. Decision log

Each decision lists the alternative considered and why it was rejected. Decisions were made during the analysis; this log was consolidated on 25 November 2026.

| # | Decision | Alternative | Why | Effect on results |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Stakeholder is the e-commerce category and sales lead | No stakeholder defined | The brief requires one. Role is an assumption and needs mentor confirmation. | Shapes the metrics chosen: margin by category and return rate |
| D2 | Headline profit is net profit (non-returned orders only), $908,214 | Use source total of $970,019 | A refunded order should not keep its profit. The source does not adjust for returns: profit on returned orders is $61,805, and their average margin (32.5%) is higher than non-returned orders (27.9%). | Net margin 16.6%. Source-basis margin would be 16.5%. |
| D3 | Headline revenue is net revenue, $5,476,537. Gross revenue ($5,865,293) kept for reconciliation. | Gross revenue as headline | Returns reduce what the business keeps. Gross is still shown so the total can be checked back to source. | Revenue KPI is 6.6% lower than gross. |
| D4 | Return rate is measured by order count (5.5%) | By value (6.6% of gross revenue) | The brief is order-level. Value view is kept as "share of returned value" on the Category sheet. | Electronics is 63% of returned value but 7.3% of its orders. Both are shown. |
| D5 | September 2023 and September 2025 are partial months. Included in totals, excluded from trend and growth. | Include all months | A partial month creates a false drop or spike. Example: September 2025 has $91,848, and treating it as a full month would show a false fall. | Growth figures are based on 23 full months. |
| D6 | Analysis at category level, not product | Product-level ranking | `product_id` is unique on 24,912 of 34,500 rows, so a product ranking would be one order per product. | Product performance is a stated limitation (L2). |
| D7 | Customer age and gender excluded from the dashboard | Customer demographic page | For 6,391 of 7,903 customer IDs, gender differs across orders, and for 7,411 IDs, age does too. The attributes are per order, not per customer. | Planned third page dropped. Demographics can be used only as per-order context. |
| D8 | Conversion rate not calculated | Estimate from orders | No visitor or session data. Any estimate would be invented. | Limitation L1, stated on the dashboard and in the README. |
| D9 | Return-rate and profit findings described as associations | Causal claims (e.g. "shipping causes Grocery losses") | No cost-of-goods or return-reason data. Shipping costs are correlated with losses in Grocery but the cause is not tested. | Recommendations are framed as tests, not proven fixes. |
| D10 | Grocery and Toys losses treated as a shipping-economics problem, not a category problem | Stop selling Grocery | Grocery's average order is $20.21, against an average shipping cost of $6.15 per order across all categories. Toys has the same pattern at a smaller scale (21% of orders loss-making). Fixing the policy covers both. | Action 2 in the executive summary. |
| D11 | Use net basis everywhere in this package | Keep the basis of the earlier Arabic report | The earlier report used gross profit for category margins. Net basis is consistent with the headline. | Small changes: Electronics share of profit moves from 35.5% to 35.1%. Grocery profit moves from -$9,188 to -$9,067. Electronics margin stays at 10.4%. The Arabic report should be updated to match (see Next steps in the summary). |
| D12 | Seasonality described as possible, not established | "December peak repeats" | Two years are not enough. The earlier report said December peaks in both years, but in 2023 October ($262.5K) was higher than December ($255.6K). Only 2024 supports a December peak. | Claim softened in the executive summary. |
| D13 | Unique counts (customers, products) hardcoded from Python | Formulas | Excel has no COUNTUNIQUE without dynamic arrays, and a pairwise formula is too slow on 34,500 rows. | Values are marked blue, with the method stated next to them. |
| D14 | Column charts instead of pie charts for category share | Pie or donut | Shares are not independent parts of a whole that people compare easily; columns show the ranking and the margin side by side. | Used on the Overview page. |
| D15 | Validation checks written as live formulas where possible | Python-only validation | The mentor can see the check and its result in Excel without running code. Python is used only where a formula is not practical (D13). | Validation_Log has 19 checks; 6 are flagged, all with actions. |
