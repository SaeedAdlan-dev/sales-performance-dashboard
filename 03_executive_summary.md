# 03. Executive summary

**Period:** 12 Sep 2023 to 11 Sep 2025 (34,500 orders)
**Basis:** Net revenue and net profit (returned orders excluded). Figures reconcile to `Project1_KPI_Validation.xlsx`.

## 1. Headline

| KPI | Value |
|---|---|
| Net revenue | $5.48M |
| Net profit | $908K |
| Net profit margin | 16.6% |
| Return rate (orders) | 5.5% |
| Returned value | $389K (6.6% of gross revenue) |
| Average order value (gross) | $170 |

## 2. What the data shows

**Electronics carries the business but has the lowest margin of any profitable category.** It is 56% of net revenue and 35% of net profit, at a 10.4% margin. It also accounts for 63% of returned value, with a 7.3% return rate by orders. Home, by comparison, earns 24.4%.

**Grocery and Toys lose money on small orders.** Grocery's average order is $20.21. Shipping costs 19% of its gross revenue. Overall, 95.5% of Grocery orders are loss-making and its net margin is -11.2%. Toys shows the same pattern at a smaller scale: 21% of its orders are loss-making and shipping is 14.5% of its revenue.

**Fashion returns are the highest by order count.** Fashion's return rate is 8.3%. Its margin is 27.3%, so reducing returns would convert a strong margin category into a stronger one.

**Returns do not vary by region or payment method.** Regional return rates range from 5.1% to 5.9%. The category is the only factor in this data that shows a clear difference.

**Growth is flat with one exception.** Excluding the two partial months, December 2024 was the year's peak at $278K. August 2025 is 14.7% above August 2024, but August 2024 was the weakest month of 2024, so most of that gain reflects a weak base. Year-on-year growth in most of 2025 stays within about ±6%. With two years of data, a seasonal pattern cannot be confirmed.

## 3. Actions

**Action 1: Protect Electronics margin.** Electronics has the largest revenue and the lowest margin of any category that makes money. Each one-point reduction in its return rate protects about $33K of revenue (1% of its $3.32M gross). Start by collecting return reasons for Electronics, then review product descriptions and quality checks for the top-returned items. In parallel, open supplier cost discussions. *Success measure:* Electronics net margin above 10.4% in the next two review periods.

**Action 2: Fix the shipping economics on small orders in Grocery and Toys.** Test a minimum order value or a free-shipping threshold in one region for one month. Track the loss-making share of orders for Grocery (currently 95.5%) and Toys (currently 21%). Do not drop Grocery as a category: the problem is the shipping policy, and it appears in Toys as well. *Success measure:* loss-making share of Grocery orders falls below 50% in the test region.

**Action 3: Reduce Fashion returns.** Fashion's return rate of 8.3% is the highest by order count, and its 27.3% margin makes each avoided return valuable. Start with size guides and product photos, then measure the return rate per month. *Success measure:* Fashion return rate below 6% over three months.

## 4. Limitations and next steps

**Limitations.** Conversion rate is not calculated because the data has no visitor or session counts. Product-level performance is not analysed, because each product ID appears almost once. Customer age and gender are not used, because they change from order to order for the same customer. The data has no cost-of-goods or return-reason fields, so causes of losses are associations, not proven.

**Next steps, in priority order:**
1. Confirm the data source. If it is a public or synthetic dataset, state that in every external use.
2. Obtain cost-of-goods and return-reason data. This is the single change that would turn the findings into a full margin analysis.
3. Obtain visitor or session data so conversion can be measured.
4. Update the earlier Arabic report to use net profit, so it matches this summary (the category figures shift slightly; see decision log D11).
